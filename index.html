Research

The batch size is the risk measure

Deep hedging trains a neural network to hedge an option by minimizing risk, one minibatch at a time. My paper shows that the batch size quietly changes what the network is minimizing.The Batch Size Is Part of the Risk Measure: Entropic Deep Hedging with an Explicit Sample Size. Sole author. NeurIPS 2026 Workshop poster, Atlanta, December 2026.

The fix. Put the batch size into the risk measure and the bias disappears. TRACE, my multi-agent hedger, then learns when to sit still.

In plain English

Train a hedging model in batches and the batch size changes the target. I showed why, fixed it, and built a system that finds the best hedge for every level of trading cost. When trading gets expensive, it stops over-trading.

As a Graduate RA in Statistics under Prof. Choudur Lakshminarayan, since January 2026 and ongoing, I study why portfolio risk breaks for volatile, fat tailed assets. Across 50 energy portfolios I run GMV optimization, VaR at several confidence levels, normality tests on the tails and demonstrations of how unstable the covariance becomes. I also lead four undergraduate RAs and wrote their teaching guide.

Why it is hard: the Bahadur term

VaR is a quantile, and the Bahadur representation says exactly how wrong a sample quantile is:

ξ̂p = ξp + (p − Fn(ξp)) / f(ξp) + Rn

The remainder Rn dies off like n−3/4 up to log factors, so the middle term is the error: its standard deviation is √(p(1 − p)/n) / f(ξp). Two things blow it up at once.

Fat tails. Deep in the tail the density f(ξp) is tiny, so 1/f is huge. At the same volatility, a Student t with 3 degrees of freedom leaves a 99% VaR estimate 2.6× noisier than the normal model, from the same data.

n close to d. With d assets and n days, the sample covariance spreads its eigenvalues over [(1 − √(d/n))², (1 + √(d/n))²]. As n → d the smallest one goes to zero, S−1 explodes, GMV weights go extreme and out of sample variance inflates by roughly 1/(1 − d/n).

Put together, the number a risk desk wants most is the hardest one to estimate, built from the noisiest weights you can make.

Projects

Built under σ > 0

Seven projects, each set by a market, a client or a clock. First, the market itself:

Paper traded on Kalshi

Prediction market prices overshoot, then drift back, so the first model I reached for was Ornstein Uhlenbeck, dX = θ(μ − X)dt + σ dW, with a fade when the price strays too far. Reversion speed θ = 3.0 a day, so a shock halves in 5.5 h. Enter at 1.5σ from the mean, exit at the mean.

Then I tested the obvious strategy on 749 settled Kalshi markets, and it lost money: contracts far from 50¢ are more accurate, not less. What worked was calibration. KalshiQuant, the public repo, has isotonic calibration fitted on the settlement tape (Brier 0.046), fee aware sizing, a Bernoulli copula CVaR in place of Gaussian VaR, vol scaled triple barrier exits, a Heston pricer for short dated binaries, a look ahead bias scanner and 160 passing tests. It runs against the live Kalshi feed in paper mode only; live trading is off by design.
